### 2.1 On real 12-lead ECGs (PTB-XL) — candidates v0.4–v0.6

The 12-lead review uses the same model per lead. PTB-XL technicians noted baseline drift, static and burst noise and electrode problems per lead (parsed in `data/ptbxl_noise.py`; weak labels). On validation-fold ECGs without any note, v0.3 rated 19% of leads noisy (limb leads up to 46%; 76% of ECGs had at least one 'noisy' lead), driven by small high-frequency hiss on low-amplitude limb leads. Candidates: v0.4 = v0.3 recipe on 40 Hz low-passed input; v0.5 = v0.4 + clean PTB-XL 12-lead leads as training bases; v0.6 = v0.3's weights with a 40 Hz input filter, no retraining (suggested by a diagnostic on validation ECGs, so it also needed a test-fold confirmation).

Rule, fixed before training: clean-lead 'not good' ≤ 5.0%; 12-lead synthetic unusable and not-good sensitivity no more than 2.0% below v0.3; MIT-BIH validation macro-F1 no more than 0.02 below; CinC 'too noisy' recall no more than 5.0% below; clean CinC rated unusable no more than 0.5% above; technician-note AUC no more than 0.03 below.

| v0.3 | 19.1% | 76.2% | 85.9% | 86.1% | 0.745 | 0.867 | 80.2% | current |
| v0.4 | 14.8% | 67.0% | 96.5% | 87.6% | 0.712 | 0.876 | 90.6% | no |

**None adopted; v0.3 stays.** Retraining on filtered input (v0.4, v0.5) catches far more real and injected noise, but still flags about 15% of un-annotated limb leads. Filtering v0.3's input (v0.6) removes the false flags but misses half of unusable leads. The limit is the label: 'noisy' is defined by injected noise power, not by whether a cardiologist can read the lead. A 12-lead quality model needs clinician-labelled leads (open issue 17).

## 4. Rhythm classifier (AI model #3)

Trained on CinC 2017 handheld single-lead ECGs, tested on held-out CinC records (stratified by record; the dataset has no patient IDs) and on MIT-BIH Holter recordings. Record-level CinC score = average of 30 s windows; challenge F1 = mean F1 of normal, AF and other rhythm. The best 2017 challenge entries scored about 83% on their hidden test set (not directly comparable).

**What happened with the AF false-alarm fix (issue 5):**

- **v0.2** (ectopy-aware features + MIT-BIH training windows) passed every validation check (false-AF windows 38 → 3, no AF lost), and on the unseen DS2 patients cut false-AF windows 106 → 25. But it found AF in only 77% of AF windows (v0.1: 97%), missing most of record 221 (AF with frequent PVCs: 18 of 50 windows). It learned a shortcut, "many PVCs means not AF", from 624 PVC-rich training windows that contained no AF. **Not adopted.**
- **v0.3** (v0.1 + an ectopy gate: an AF call is withdrawn when the rhythm becomes regular once PVC-adjacent beats are removed; gate only on lead MLII/II, where the beat classifier is validated) cut validation false AF 38 → 6 with no AF lost, but on DS2 found AF in only 65% of AF windows. AF irregularity varies a lot between patients (PVC-free RR variation median 0.22 in validation patient 201, 0.14 in test patient 210), and a threshold chosen on one AF patient does not transfer. **Not adopted.**
- **Pipeline keeps v0.1**: it catches ~97% of AF but raises more false alarms, which go to a reader for review. Missing AF is the more harmful error for this product.
- **Lessons:** the validation set had a single AF patient, too few to choose anything that trades AF sensitivity; and DS2 has now informed two decisions, so it is no longer an unbiased final test. Both are fixed by an AF validation set with many AF patients (MIT-BIH AF Database, 23 patients) and a fresh external test set.

**Retry with the MIT-BIH AF Database** (23 Holter patients, ~10 h each): patients ranked by AF burden and dealt into train 8 / validation 8 / test 7. The AFDB test patients were not used for any decision.

Selection rule (fixed before any AFDB result): Adopt a candidate over v0.1 only if, on VALIDATION data: (1) AF sensitivity drops by at most 2 points on AFDB-val and on MIT-BIH-val, and no AFDB-val AF patient's AF sensitivity drops by more than 10 points; (2) CinC val challenge F1 drops by at most 1 point; (3) false-AF windows on AFDB-val + MIT-BIH-val are fewer than v0.1's. Among passing candidates, fewest false-AF windows wins. Fixed before any AFDB result.

### 7.2 Interval measurements (signal processing) — adopted version v0.2

Median beat per lead → QRS onset/offset from the 12-lead spatial velocity (QRS core, then out to a threshold or the first velocity minimum) → T end where the 12-lead RMS returns to baseline (capped 30 ms after the tangent-method T end) → P wave searched up to 300 ms before the QRS. Thresholds tuned on 1,000 training-fold ECGs against GE 12SL. Test fold: 2,198 ECGs, 0 not measurable, 11 ms per ECG on one CPU core.

**No ground truth exists here.** PTB-XL+ supplies the measurements of two commercial algorithms (GE 12SL, Uni-G). The useful yardstick is how closely the two commercial algorithms agree with each other.

## 8. Issues found while testing

### Fixed
1. **Beat positions ~40 ms late.** Pan-Tompkins marks beats after the R peak; the classifier is trained on R-peak positions. The first end-to-end run agreed with the cardiologists on only 42% of beats in a test minute. Every detection is now refined to the R peak (`qrs.refine_peaks`): 92% on the same minute. Regression test added.
2. **Quality gate hid arrhythmic, low-amplitude signal.** With quality v0.1, record 222 (R amplitude 0.68 mV against a 1.45 mV median; flutter, AF and nodal-rhythm episodes) had ~half its windows rated unusable, and 8–16% of clean expert-labelled handheld ECG windows were rated unusable. v0.2 (AF/other-rhythm windows as good, low-amplitude examples) fixed that but became lenient on real noisy recordings; v0.3 adds real noisy windows. Chosen on validation: beats hidden in unusable segments across the 22 test patients fell from 1,839 to about 250, record 222 from 1,173 to about 70.
3. **False beat at the start of every recording.** Pan-Tompkins' thresholds are still settling in the first moments (record 221: detection at 0.32 s, first real beat at 0.61 s). Detections in the first 0.4 s are now dropped. Test added.
4. **'Supraventricular ectopic beats' reported during AF.** Premature atrial beats are not defined in AF; that finding is now suppressed when AF is the predominant rhythm.
5. **Resampling mixed up the leads of a 12-lead ECG.** The resampling helper worked along the first array axis, so a (12 leads × samples) ECG was resampled across leads instead of over time. Found by the first 12-lead end-to-end run (training was not affected: it uses the database's own 100 Hz files). It now always works along time; single-lead results are unchanged (demo record 233 reproduced exactly). Test added.
6. **12-lead measurements were reported for pure noise.** When every lead is rated unusable, measurements and statements are now both withheld. Test added.
7. **12-lead measurement v0.1 had large misses** (wide QRS, low P waves, flat T waves; section 7.2). Fixed in v0.2.
7b. **Statement model v0.2 learned 'no PVC' from 27,901 Ningbo ECGs** because the label map used a PVC code that dataset never uses (section 7.5). Fixed in v0.3 with a map built from the dataset's actual code list; Chapman PVC AUC 0.907 → 0.990. Lesson, now a rule: before training on a new dataset, list every label code it actually uses and check each mapped statement has positives.

### Open (next work)
8. **AF over-called in ectopy-rich recordings — four candidates tried, none adopted** (section 4). Every candidate cut false AF substantially but lost real AF in some patient. With the MIT-BIH AF Database (8 validation AF patients) the pre-declared rule now catches this on validation. On fresh AFDB test patients, v0.1 itself finds 97.4% of AF windows with 95.5% positive predictivity, so the false-alarm problem is concentrated in non-AF patients with frequent ectopy (MIT-BIH DS2: PPV ~60%). Next options: (a) an explicit AF-probability operating point chosen on validation per use case (screening vs. review) — a transparent sensitivity/false-alarm trade-off; (b) more AF data (Long-Term AF Database, 84 patients); (c) beat labels inside AF for a beat classifier that is reliable during AF.
8b. **The beat classifier labels some AF beats as ventricular**, on handheld lead I especially (a quarter of CinC AF windows had ≥10% 'PVCs'). Anything built on its PVC labels must be restricted to leads where it is validated.
9. **Supraventricular (S) and fusion (F) beats are weak** (S Se ~30%, F ~0%). The v0.2 augmentation recipe scored lower on validation and was not adopted. Next: more S/F data (INCART, long-term AF database), patient-adaptive RR features.
10. **Beat-model calibration does not transfer across patients.** A temperature fitted on the 4 validation patients made test ECE worse (slightly under-confident). The rhythm model's calibration did improve on test. Next: calibrate beats on a larger, more varied patient set; until then beat confidence levels must not be shown as calibrated in any UI.
11. **Proxy and weak labels.** Synthetic quality labels come from known noise levels; CinC 'too noisy' labels are per recording, not per window. Clinician-labelled windows are needed.
12. **Public data only.** MIT-BIH (1970s Holter, MLII) and CinC 2017 (handheld, lead I). Performance on modern 12-lead carts and OT/ICU monitors is unknown until tested on partner-hospital data.
13. **CinC split is by record, not patient** (no patient IDs are published), so some test records may share a patient with training records. The cross-dataset MIT-BIH result is the stronger generalisation evidence.
14. **Rare 12-lead statements are still weak.** Atrial flutter: Chapman sensitivity 9% (no trustworthy flutter training labels: Ningbo's are mislabelled fibrillation). Long QT: AUC 0.93 on Chapman but sensitivity 22% at the PTB-XL threshold. WPW has 8 PTB-XL test ECGs. Per-site thresholds raise flutter sensitivity to about 67% but at about 22% PPV (section 7.7), and atrial-activity features (section 11) did not clear the pre-declared bar, so flutter must not be claimed. Next: flutter data with verified labels (e.g. CODE-15%, MIMIC-IV-ECG; licences in PRODUCT_PLAN §7).
15. **Sex gap in the 12-lead model.** Superclass macro-AUC on PTB-XL test: v0.1 0.914 women vs 0.935 men; v0.3 0.917 vs 0.939 — more data did not close it. Section 7.6: the gap is myocardial infarction (validation, age 40–80: about 0.905 women vs 0.941 men); other superclasses are even. Subgroup results must be part of every future model card.
16. **12-lead measurements are checked against other algorithms, not a referee.** IEC 60601-2-25 requires the CSE database (licensed). PR is not measured beyond 300 ms (very long first-degree AV block) and paced ECGs are not handled specially.
17. **The signal-quality model was trained on Holter lead II, not 12-lead ECGs.** On clean PTB-XL validation ECGs it rates 0.35% of leads unusable and 19% noisy (76% of ECGs show a 'noisy' lead). Three 12-lead candidates were rejected by a pre-declared rule (section 2.1). Next: clinician-labelled 12-lead leads (readable / noisy / unusable); until then the review screen should show 'noisy' as a soft hint, not a warning.
18. **Two countries, no Indian or US data yet.** PTB-XL (Schiller devices, Germany, 1989–1996) and Chapman/Ningbo (China, 2013–2018). Performance on today's ECG carts in Indian and US hospitals is unknown until tested on partner data.
19. **Thresholds are per site.** The same model needs different yes/no thresholds where hospitals label differently (section 7.3–7.4). Tool and simulated installation: section 7.7. The engine loads a signed-off site configuration (`cardiotic-engine site-config` drafts it; the cardiology lead approves it).

## 11. Atrial flutter: can signal processing help?

Flutter is the weakest statement (Chapman sensitivity 9% at the PTB-XL threshold). Atrial-activity features (QRST cancellation, then spectral peak and organisation at 3–10 Hz in II, aVF and V1; `ecg12/flutter.py`) were added to the model's own flutter and AF probabilities in a logistic regression. Rule, fixed before any result: adopt if 5-fold cross-validated F1 on the development half of Chapman–Shaoxing (5,322 ECGs, 236 with flutter) beats the best F1 of the model's flutter probability alone by at least 0.1.

Development half: detector F1 0.438 vs model alone 0.346 (gain +0.091). **Not adopted.**

Held-out half (5,323 ECGs, 209 with flutter), scored once:

| | AUC | Sensitivity | PPV | F1 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Detector | 0.928 | 54.1% | 30.2% | 0.388 |
| Model alone, best threshold | 0.903 | 67.5% | 22.2% | 0.335 |
| Model, PTB-XL threshold | — | 11.0% | 25.6% | 0.154 |

The features help a little (AUC 0.90 → 0.93) but not enough to claim flutter detection: at about 54% sensitivity the PPV is about 30%. Flutter stays out of the product's claims; the per-site thresholds (§7.7) remain the way to trade sensitivity against false alarms.
